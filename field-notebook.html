Tree or building height

Stand a known distance away, aim the phone at the top, and the angle gives the height. No tape up the tree needed.

Paces fill the distance for you. Measure your pace once by walking 10 paces on a tape and dividing by 10.

How to measure

  1. Stand on level ground and measure the distance to the foot of the tree or wall, or count paces.
  2. Press Start motion sensor and allow access. Hold the phone steady with both hands.
  3. Aim at the top, then press Capture top and keep still for two seconds.
  4. Check the result and save it. If the ground slopes, tick the box and capture the base angle too.
  5. 45° shortcut: walk backwards until the angle reads 45° (the box turns green). The height is then the distance to the object plus your eye height.

Phone angle sensors are rarely better than about a degree or two, and the stated ± assumes that. A tree leaning toward or away from you, or a top you cannot see clearly, adds error. Measure from two sides and average for better results.

Strike and dip

Lay the phone flat, screen up, on a planar surface such as a bedding plane, joint or fault. The phone reads the surface’s tilt and the direction it faces.

How to measure

  1. Press Start motion sensor. Keep the phone away from steel, rebar, magnets and phone-case clasps, which bend the compass.
  2. Lay the phone flat on the rock surface with the screen facing up. The surface should be a smooth plane, not a rough bump.
  3. Wait for the numbers to settle, then press Capture and keep the phone still for three seconds.
  4. The result is shown as strike/dip with the dip direction, for example 045/30 SE, using the right-hand rule: looking along the strike, the surface dips to your right.

Compass readings on phones can be off by several degrees, and more near magnetic rocks or metal. Take several readings on the same surface and compare them in the Data tab. On iPhone the direction is less exact than on many Android phones.
