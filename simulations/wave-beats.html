Wave beats

Add two waves of nearly the same frequency and watch the sum swell and fade. The slow rise and fall is called a beat, and its rate is the difference between the two frequencies. Change the frequencies and amplitudes, then listen to it.

Listening starts quietly. Turn your device volume down first, then raise it slowly.

The idea

The two waves are y₁ = A₁ sin(2πf₁t) and y₂ = A₂ sin(2πf₂t), and the sum is y₁ + y₂. When the waves are in step the peaks add and the sound is loud. A little later the peaks of one line up with the troughs of the other and they partly cancel. The sum's strength swings between A₁ + A₂ and |A₁ − A₂|.

The beat frequency is fbeat = |f₂ − f₁|. If both amplitudes are equal the sound fades to silence between beats. The ear hears a single tone at the average frequency (f₁ + f₂) ÷ 2 that gets louder and softer. Tuning two instruments until the beats slow down and stop is a classic way to bring them to the same pitch. Beating is also how a radar speed gun and many interferometers read very small frequency differences.

The picture is drawn to a time scale that shows about three beats. If the carrier would be too dense to see (frequency much larger than the difference), the drawn wave uses fewer cycles than the real sound, and the note under the chart says so. The beat rate and the amplitudes are always exact. Listening uses the real frequencies you set. Wave 2 is always the higher one.
