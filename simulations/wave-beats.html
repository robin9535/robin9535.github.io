Equations with your numbers

General rule: sin a + sin b = 2 sin((a+b)/2) cos((a−b)/2). With equal amplitudes and no phase difference, the sum is a fast wave at the average frequency whose size is shaped by a slow cosine. That slow shape is the envelope. Loudness peaks twice per cosine cycle, so the beat frequency is |f₂ − f₁|.

The idea

When the two waves are in step the peaks add and the sound is loud. A little later the peaks of one line up with the troughs of the other and they partly cancel. The sum swings between A₁ + A₂ and |A₁ − A₂|. With equal amplitudes it falls to silence between beats.

The ear hears one tone at the average frequency (f₁ + f₂) ÷ 2 that gets louder and softer at the beat frequency. Tuning two instruments until the beats slow down and stop is a classic way to bring them to the same pitch.

In the 3-beat overview the carrier waves would be too dense to see, so they are drawn with fewer cycles than the real sound, and a note under the chart says so. The beat rate, amplitudes and envelope are always exact. The other windows use the real frequencies.
