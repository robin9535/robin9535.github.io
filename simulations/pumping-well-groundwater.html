How it works

The Theis (1935) solution gives the drawdown s at distance r after pumping a confined aquifer at a constant rate Q for time t:

s = Q / (4πT) · W(u),   u = r²S / (4Tt)

W(u) is the well function (the exponential integral E1(u)). With two wells, the drawdowns simply add (superposition). The radius of influence shown is the Cooper–Jacob estimate r = 1.5·√(Tt/S), the distance beyond which drawdown is negligible.

Limits. The model assumes a uniform, flat, confined aquifer of infinite extent, fully penetrating wells, constant pumping and no recharge or leakage. Real aquifers in Bangladesh are layered and recharged each monsoon, so use it to understand the behaviour, not to design a well field. For an unconfined aquifer it is only valid while the drawdown is small compared with the saturated thickness.

Hand pumps. A suction-mode hand tubewell can lift water only from about 7 m below ground in practice, so if the water level falls deeper than that the pump stops delivering water even though the aquifer still holds it. This is the reason deeper-set pumps are used in some areas. The page flags this using 7 m as a round-number limit.
