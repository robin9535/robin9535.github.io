Projectile Motion

Throw a ball at any speed, angle and height and watch the path it takes. Earlier shots stay on screen, so you can test what really changes the range.

Set up the throw

The dashed line is the predicted path. Each launch leaves a faded trace, so you can compare shots.

Try this

  • From ground level, find the angle that gives the longest range. Then launch at 30° and at 60° and compare where they land.
  • Raise the launch height to 30 m. Is 45° still the best angle?
  • Keep speed and angle fixed and switch to the Moon. By what factor does the range change, and why that number?
  • Watch the two arrows on the ball. Which one never changes during the flight?

The equations

x = u cos θ · t
y = h + u sin θ · t − ½ g t²
On level ground: R = u² sin 2θ / g,  H = u² sin² θ / 2g,  T = 2u sin θ / g

Air resistance is ignored, as in the textbook treatment. A real ball lands shorter than this, and its best angle is a little under 45°.
