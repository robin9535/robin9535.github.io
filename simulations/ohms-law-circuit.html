Ohm's Law Circuit

Connect a resistor and a bulb to a battery, in series or in parallel. Change the voltage and the resistances and watch the current and the brightness respond.

Build the circuit

Dots move the way conventional current flows, from the positive plate round to the negative. Faster dots mean more current. The glow shows the power in the bulb.

Try this

  • In series, double the battery voltage. What happens to the current? Check the graph: why is it a straight line through zero?
  • In series, make the resistor bigger. The bulb dims. Now do the same in parallel. Why does the bulb not care?
  • Switch from series to parallel without touching anything else. Does the battery supply more current or less?
  • In parallel, add the two branch currents. Compare with the current from the battery.

The equations

V = I R
Series: R = R₁ + R₂
Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂
P = V I

The bulb is treated as a fixed resistance and the battery and wires as having none. A real filament's resistance rises as it heats up, so a real bulb's graph bends.
