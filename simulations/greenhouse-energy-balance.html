Greenhouse energy balance

See why Earth is warm enough for life. Balance sunlight coming in against heat going out, then strengthen the greenhouse effect, change how reflective the planet is, or dim the Sun, and watch the temperature respond over the years.

Controls

0 = no greenhouse gases, 1 = all surface heat is absorbed. About 0.78 reproduces Earth's present mean surface temperature in this simple model.
Deeper means the planet warms or cools more slowly.

The starting point is the equilibrium of the settings you had when you last pressed the button (or the present-day settings). The chart then shows the global mean surface temperature relaxing to the new equilibrium.

How it works

  • Sunlight in. The Sun's energy is spread over a sphere, so the average arriving at the top of the atmosphere is S/4. A fraction α (albedo) is reflected; the rest, A = S(1 − α)/4, is absorbed.
  • No atmosphere. The surface must radiate A back out: σT⁴ = A. The result is the effective temperature Te, the temperature seen from space.
  • One-layer atmosphere. The atmosphere lets sunlight through, absorbs a fraction ε of the heat radiated by the surface and re-emits it equally up and down. Solving the two energy balances gives σTs⁴ = A / (1 − ε/2). The downward part is the "back radiation" that warms the surface.
  • Response in time. The surface temperature changes at the rate C·dT/dt = A − (1 − ε/2)σT⁴, where C = ρ c h is the heat capacity of a water layer of depth h. Moving a slider and pressing the button shows how long adjustment takes.
  • Ice–albedo feedback (illustrative). When switched on, albedo rises linearly from your slider value at 280 K and above to 0.62 at 255 K and below, a made-up ramp to show how warming melts ice, lowers albedo and warms further. The equilibrium shown is the warm state found by starting from a warm planet; a real, strongly cooled planet could settle in a different (icier) state.

This is a teaching model of the global average. It has no clouds, no water-vapour feedback, no ocean circulation and no seasons, and a single ε stands in for a very complex atmosphere. It illustrates the physics; it does not predict real-world warming for a given amount of CO₂.
