Stress and Strain: Hooke’s Law

Stretch a wire step by step. See where Hooke’s law holds, where the stretch becomes permanent, and where the wire breaks.

Try this

  • Pull a little and let go. The wire returns to its first length, so it is still elastic. The graph is a straight line.
  • Pull past the yield point and let go. The wire stays longer than before: that is the permanent set.
  • Keep pulling beyond the highest stress. Where does the wire start to thin?
  • Compare steel with copper. Which has the steeper line, and so the larger Young's modulus?
  • Try glass. Does it stretch much before it breaks?

The equations

Stress = F / A    Strain = ΔL / L
Hooke's law: stress ∝ strain
Young's modulus Y = stress / strain = FL / (AΔL)

The numbers are typical, rounded values chosen to give the right shape of curve for each material. Tables in your textbook may differ a little. The curve between the yield point and the break is drawn as a smooth model, not measured data.
