Ideal Gas: Boyle and Charles

Change the volume and temperature of a gas. The molecules, the pressure and the graph all respond together.

Pressure P
Volume V
Temperature T
P × V
Molecule rms speed
Wall pressure, measured / formula

Squeeze and heat the gas

Try this

  • Keep T. Halve the volume. The molecules hit the walls twice as often, and the pressure doubles: Boyle's law.
  • Choose “keep P” and raise the temperature. The volume grows in step with T: Charles's law.
  • Raise T at fixed V. The molecules move faster, so the pressure rises.
  • Watch “measured / formula”. It wobbles around 1 because the molecules really do produce the pressure.

The equations

PV = nRT
Boyle: PV = constant (T fixed)
Charles: V / T = constant (P fixed)
vrms = √(3RT / M)    ½ m v² = (3/2) kT

The gas is 0.1 mol of air (M = 0.029 kg/mol) and R = 8.314 J/mol K. The picture is a flat, slowed-down model with 70 molecules and no collisions between them, as in the ideal gas.
