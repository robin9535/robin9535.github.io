Pumping well drawdown

Pump a well and watch the water table sink into a cone of depression that spreads outward with time. Change the pumping rate and the aquifer properties, add a second well to see how wells interfere, and read the drawdown at any distance.

How it works

  • Theis equation (1935). For a well pumped at a constant rate Q in a confined aquifer, the drawdown s at distance r after time t is s = Q / (4πT) · W(u), with u = r²S / (4Tt). W(u) is the well function, W(u) = −0.5772 − ln u + u − u²/(2·2!) + u³/(3·3!) − …
  • Cone of depression. Drawdown is largest at the well and falls with distance. The cone keeps widening as long as pumping continues.
  • Radius of influence. The Cooper–Jacob approximation r0 = 1.5 √(T t / S) gives the distance at which the straight-line approximation of drawdown reaches zero. It is valid once u is small.
  • Two wells. Drawdowns simply add (superposition): s = s1(r1) + s2(r2), where r1 and r2 are the distances to each well. The cross-section runs along the line through both wells.

The model assumes a confined, uniform, infinite aquifer of constant thickness, a fully penetrating well with negligible storage, and constant pumping, with no leakage or boundaries. Real Bangladesh aquifers are layered and leaky, so this explains the behaviour but must not be used to design or license a well. Drawdown shown is at the aquifer level; the vertical scale is exaggerated to make the cone visible.
