Try this

  • Double the distance from 100 m to 200 m. How many decibels do you lose? Try 400 m to 800 m.
  • Go from one machine to two. The level does not double. By how much does it rise, and from two to four?
  • Set the background to 55 dB in a residential zone. Can any distance meet the day limit? What does that tell you about the baseline?
  • Find the smallest barrier loss that lets a house at 150 m meet the night limit.

The equations

L₂ = L₁ − 20 log₁₀ (r₂ / r₁)
n equal sources: L = L₁ + 10 log₁₀ n
Adding levels: L = 10 log₁₀ (10La/10 + 10Lb/10)

This is the point-source rule for open, flat ground: 6 dB lost for each doubling of distance. It leaves out ground and air absorption, reflections, wind and the exact geometry of a barrier, so treat it as a first screening estimate. Limits shown are the dB(A) values in Bangladesh's Noise Pollution (Control) Rules 2006; check the current rules before quoting them in a report.
