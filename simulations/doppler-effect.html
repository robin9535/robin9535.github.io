Doppler Effect

Slide the source speed up and watch the wavefronts. Compare the pitch heard ahead of the source and behind it.

Try this

  • Start with speed 0. The circles are evenly spaced and both listeners hear the true frequency.
  • Raise the speed. Ahead of the source the waves bunch up (higher pitch). Behind it they spread out (lower pitch).
  • Set the speed to 340 m/s, the speed of sound. The waves pile up in front of the source.
  • Go above 340 m/s. The circles form a cone, a shock wave. The simple formula no longer applies.

The equations

Source moving toward listener: f′ = f v / (v − vs)
Source moving away: f′ = f v / (v + vs)
λ′ = (v − vs) / f   (ahead)
Cone: sin θ = v / vs   (vs > v)

The speed of sound is taken as 340 m/s. Listeners are at rest. The picture is slowed down and the number of circles per second is fixed, so the spacing shows the effect clearly. The frequencies in the boxes use the real numbers.
