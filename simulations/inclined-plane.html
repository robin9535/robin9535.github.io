Inclined Plane and Friction

Raise the slope until the block slips. Watch the weight split into a part along the plane and a part into it, and see friction answer.

Try this

  • Raise the angle slowly while the block rests. Friction grows to match mg sinθ until the block just starts to slip.
  • Find the angle where it slips, then compare with tan-1μs, the angle of repose.
  • Double the mass. Does the slipping angle change? Does the acceleration change?
  • Set μk to 0.05 on a steep plane and compare with a rough one.

The equations

N = mg cosθ    mg sinθ pulls down the slope
At rest: f = mg sinθ   (while f ≤ μsN)
Sliding: a = g (sinθ − μk cosθ)
Angle of repose: tan θ = μs

Uses g = 9.8 m/s². The block starts near the top of a 3 m slope and stops when it reaches the bottom. Needs μk ≤ μs to make sense, so the sliders keep that order.
