    How this works, and its limits

    1. Boundaries come from OpenStreetMap (searched through Nominatim) or from what you draw or upload. Named areas can differ slightly from official survey boundaries.
    2. Area and perimeter are calculated on the globe from the boundary shape, in your browser.
    3. Elevation is read from a global digital elevation model (about 90 m grid, through Open-Meteo) at sample points inside the area. Minimum and maximum are therefore approximate. Much of Bangladesh is very flat, so differences are small.
    4. Pollution is a modelled estimate (Copernicus CAMS through Open-Meteo), not a ground measurement. The model grid is coarse, roughly tens of kilometres, so small areas often show nearly the same value everywhere. Use it for a regional picture, and use satellite or monitor data for local detail.
    5. Your drawn or uploaded shapes stay in your browser. Only the sample coordinates are sent to the data services.
