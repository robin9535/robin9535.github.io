Air dispersion model

Estimate ground-level pollutant concentration downwind of a stack or chimney with the Gaussian plume model: the peak value and where it lands, the concentration at any receptor, and a plan-view map of the plume. A standard environmental engineering calculation for a brick kiln, factory or generator exhaust.

Overcast conditions, day or night, are class D. This page merges A and B into one class.

The equation

Ground-level concentration with total reflection from the ground, for a continuous point source:

C(x, y, 0) = Q / (π · u · σy · σz) · exp(−y² / 2σy²) · exp(−H² / 2σz²)

C in g/m³ (shown in µg/m³), Q in g/s, u in m/s, H the effective release height in m. The dispersion widths σy and σz (m) depend on downwind distance x and stability class and are taken from the Briggs (1973) formulas for open country and urban surroundings. The peak is found by scanning x from 20 m to 20 km.

Limits. Steady wind and emission, flat terrain, no chemical reaction, no deposition, and it is a screening estimate that works best from about 100 m to 10 km. Buildings, hills, sea breezes and calm conditions are not modelled, and real concentrations can differ from the result by a factor of two or more. Use it to learn and to compare scenarios, not as a regulatory assessment.
